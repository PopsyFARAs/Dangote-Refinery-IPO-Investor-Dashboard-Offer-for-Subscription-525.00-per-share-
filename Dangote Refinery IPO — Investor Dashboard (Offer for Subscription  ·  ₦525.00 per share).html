Where the money goes

Net proceeds of ₦2,111.0bn applied to the refinery expansion programme.
The full expansion programme is costed at approximately US$14.3bn. The Offer funds a portion; the balance comes from operating cash flow, debt, trade and project finance.

The case the prospectus makes

  • Scale with complexity. 700,000 bpd nameplate at a Nelson Complexity Index of 11.5, against a weighted average of 8.9 for emerging-market refineries
  • Demonstrated ramp. Performance testing at 650,000 bpd in February 2026; stable full-capacity operation from March 2026; up to 700,000 bpd achieved in June 2026
  • Margin inflection already banked. Gross margin moved from −9.5% in FY2024 to +1.9% in FY2025 to +17.9% in H1 2026
  • Dollar-linked revenue. US Dollar functional and presentation currency, insulating reported results from Naira translation on the revenue line
  • Domestic position. 100% of domestically produced PMS and approximately 87.6% of total Nigerian PMS supply including imports, as at 31 May 2026
  • Low leverage at the point of listing. Net debt to EBITDA of 0.27× at 30 June 2026
  • Free zone fiscal position. OGFZA framework incentives, subject to the 25% customs-territory sales condition and the 1 January 2028 change

The case the risk factors make

  • Six months is a short track record. The profitable period is H1 2026 alone. FY2024 and FY2025 were both loss-making at the bottom line
  • Single-asset concentration. One refinery, one site, one crude distillation unit. A single unplanned outage hits the entire revenue base
  • Margin, not volume, is the driver. Profitability is the crack spread. Neither crude cost nor product price is within the Issuer’s control
  • Tax cliff. From 1 January 2028, profits on sales into the Nigerian customs territory may become taxable
  • Thin float. 3.30% free float raises the risk of illiquidity, wide spreads and price volatility after listing
  • Not underwritten, 50% minimum. The Offer can fail to reach its threshold; monies are returned with uncertain timing
  • Expansion is unbuilt. US$14.3bn of capital expenditure, targeted 2029–2030, subject to approvals, financing and execution

Free zone incentives

Principal incentives available under the OGFZ Act and the Nigeria Tax Act.

The 2028 condition. The incentives depend on not more than 25% of sales going into the Nigerian customs territory. From 1 January 2028, profits on customs- territory sales may become subject to Nigerian tax.

Settlement mechanics

  • Successful applicants’ CSCS accounts credited within 15 business days of allotment
  • Listing and commencement of trading, 15 business days after allotment
  • Surplus and rejected application monies returned within 5 business days
  • Without valid CHN and CSCS details, shares are warehoused at CSCS under RIN in the Registrar’s custody until details are supplied

Allotment methodology

  • The Issuer sets a Full Allotment Threshold — the highest application size at which every valid application can be met in full
  • All valid applications at or below that threshold are allotted in full
  • The balance is allocated among larger applications on the approved basis of allotment
  • On over-subscription the Issuer may absorb up to 30% of the Offer, subject to SEC approval
  • Minimum subscription threshold for the Offer as a whole is 50%

Conclusion

This offer asks the market to price an asset that has only recently begun to behave like the thing it was built to be. For four of the six reporting periods in the prospectus, the Issuer had no revenue at all. For a fifth, it had revenue and a gross loss. The commercial history that supports a ₦65 trillion listing valuation is, in substance, the six months to 30 June 2026 — a period in which the Refinery reached stable full-capacity operation, gross margin reached 17.9%, and profit after tax of ₦2.50 trillion arrived after two consecutive loss-making years.

That is not a criticism. It is the defining fact of the analysis. Every optimistic reading of this offer rests on the proposition that H1 2026 is the new normal rather than a favourable half. Every cautious reading rests on the observation that one half-year is a thin base from which to extrapolate, in a business whose profitability is a spread between two commodity prices that management does not set.

The prospectus itself is admirably direct about this. It discloses the limited operating history at nameplate capacity, the single-train configuration, the margin dependency, the 2028 tax change, the absence of underwriting and the 50% minimum subscription threshold. It does not oversell. An investor who reads pages 134 to 155 as carefully as pages 80 to 95 will be well equipped.

What the numbers do show without ambiguity: leverage is low at the point of listing at 0.27× net debt to EBITDA; the working capital cycle is unusually tight at roughly a week; cash generation turned firmly positive; and the free float being created is small, at 3.30%, which is a liquidity consideration in both directions. Those are facts. The judgement about whether ₦525 is the right price for them is yours to make, and is properly made with a registered adviser who knows your position.

What would change the picture

Signposts worth watching after listing, in either direction.

Would strengthen the case

  • A second consecutive half-year at or near H1 2026 margins
  • Sustained throughput at 700,000 bpd through a full turnaround cycle
  • Expansion capital expenditure landing on schedule and on budget
  • Dividend declared and remitted without foreign exchange friction
  • Export share stabilising rather than continuing to decline

Would weaken it

  • Crack spread compression from global refining overcapacity
  • An unplanned outage at the single crude distillation unit
  • Adverse resolution of the customs-territory tax position before 2028
  • Expansion cost overrun requiring further equity or expensive debt
  • Persistent illiquidity in the shares on a 3.30% float
